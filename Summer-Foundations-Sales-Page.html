A More Human English Course

Rigorous enough to prepare them. Spacious enough to let the book do its work.

This is not a packet of comprehension questions and it is not literary analysis stripped of wonder. Students begin by narrating what they read, then notice what lingers, trace ideas through the novel, and shape their own response.

The writing comes after thought. The analysis grows from attention. By the end, students have practiced close reading, discussion, argument, and revision without losing the experience of the story itself.

Charlotte Mason is the root here, not the ceiling: living books, narration, real ideas, and serious upper-level thinking belong together.

A Practice of Attention

Find it. Follow it. Frame it. Keep it.

The same four movements every week — until they become the way a reader thinks.

Find It

Notice the moment that stops you — a line, an image, a choice, a question.

Follow It

Trace that thread through the book. Watch where it returns and how it deepens.

Frame It

Put words around what you found — first aloud in narration, then clearly in writing.

Keep It

Keep what became yours: the lines, the narration, the finished piece, the insight worth carrying.

Course at a Glance

The Six Weeks

One theme each week, read slowly and talked through before anything is written. Alongside it, one small capstone step builds toward a project entirely the student's own.

Week 1

The World of the Novel

The ranch, the river, the men who pass through. Meeting the place and the people who fill it.

CapstoneChoose Your Thread — select one character to follow all summer and a Relation Study to carry alongside.

Week 2

Mercy and Old Age

Candy and his dog — what a hard world does with the weak and the aging, and what mercy costs.

CapstoneA Letter from Your Character — step inside their voice and write the letter they would write.

Week 3

Outcasts and Belonging

Crooks, Curley's wife, and the ache to be seen. Who the ranch sets apart, and why.

CapstoneFreedom & the Choices We Make — where does your character have a real choice, and what does it cost?

Week 4

Endings

Dreams, and how they break; the quiet weight of what everyone can feel coming.

CapstoneRewrite the Scene — re-see one moment from the final chapter through your character's eyes.

Week 5

Second Sight

Re-read the opening and closing side by side — the book ends where it began, and now means something new.

CapstoneA Haiku for Your Character — distill the summer into seventeen syllables; Camus and Sartre join us.

Week 6

What We Carry

What the story leaves in your hands — friendship, loss, and the cost of mercy.

CapstoneArrival — bring the Relation Study home and gather the summer's work into one capstone.

Texas TEKS + Common Core alignment

Across Find · Follow · Frame · Keep, students practice comprehension, response, multiple genres, author's purpose and craft, oral language, and composition. The course maps to six of the seven major ELA strands in six weeks.

  • Find It: inference, interpretation, and textual evidence (CCSS RL.9–10.1).
  • Follow It: theme, character, and author's craft (RL.9–10.2–3).
  • Frame + Keep: discussion, argument, composition, and writing from evidence (SL.9–10.1; W.9–10.1, 9).
Learning outcomes
  • Narrate a chapter with confidence and meaningful detail.
  • Trace a theme or character thread across a whole novel.
  • Use textual evidence to support an interpretation.
  • Move from spoken narration to a clear written argument.
  • Revise a piece of writing without losing the student's own voice.
